## OVERVIEW

**bloodoath** started as a badge, a look. the work is turning it into something you can check: a seal that binds an artifact, a page or a file, to the person who made it, so anyone can verify who signed it and that nothing changed since.

it's already in use. [humanlayer](/ecosystem/sectors/humanlayer/) signs members in with a bloodoath key instead of a password, and the static sites seal every built page when a key is set. the signing code lives once, in [@dontfeedmachines/fieldkit](/ecosystem/packages/fieldkit/), and runs the same in the browser and on the server. the dedicated oath and key station lives at `bloodoath.dev`.

## HOW THE SEAL WORKS

the approach that shipped is ed25519, the nostr-style route, because it's fast, light, and has no clunky key tooling for the person signing.

1. take the page content and compute its sha-256 hash.
2. sign that hash with the author's ed25519 private key.
3. write four meta tags into the page: `bloodoath:pubkey`, `bloodoath:hash`, `bloodoath:signature`, and `bloodoath:timestamp`.
4. on load, the badge script hashes the page *as it is now*, and verifies the signature against the public key.

pass and the badge reads `[SYS.STATUS: VERIFIED HUMAN]`. fail and it flashes `[SYS.STATUS: COMPROMISED / MACHINE INTERFERENCE]`. edit a single byte after signing and the seal fractures.

## THE ROUTES THAT WERE WEIGHED

the spec looked at five ways to do this before ed25519 won. the others are still on the table as layers.

1. **pgp cleartext signatures.** the cypherpunk classic. hash the content, sign with gpg, embed the armored signature, verify in-browser against a keyserver. the most on-theme, the clunkiest to use.
2. **ed25519 / nostr keys.** what shipped.
3. **webauthn passkeys, "proof of flesh."** the signature comes from a hardware authenticator that needs a fingerprint or a button press, so producing the seal literally takes the author's body. the best narrative fit, harder to verify without a backend.
4. **ipfs content addressing.** you can't verify a page that keeps changing, so package and pin it to ipfs and sign the content id. if the loaded code differs from the cid by one byte, the seal breaks.
5. **web-of-trust signatures.** one signature proves *who* wrote something, not that the who is human. cross-sign keys between known people, like a key-signing party, and show how many hops an author sits from a verified human. this is the idea humanlayer's vouch tiers grew out of.

## WHAT IT DOESN'T PROVE

a seal proves a key signed this content and nothing changed since. it doesn't prove the key holder didn't use a model to write the thing. that gap is what the web-of-trust layer and humanlayer's vouching are for: they tie the key to a person other people will stand behind.

## STATUS

ed25519 signing and verification are built and running in humanlayer and the static-site builds, through `@noble/ed25519`. passkey, ipfs, and web-of-trust layers are ideas in the spec, not built.
